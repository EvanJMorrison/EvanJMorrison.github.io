I find myself in an odd place when writing this review. On the one hand, I think this book is a 3/5. On the other hand, I think it’s well worth the read and would highly recommend it. The struggle between those two positions illustrates both the strengths and weaknesses of _She Who Became the Sun_. The characters, especially Zhu, are fantastic, but the writing about them is not.

You meet Zhu as a child and see her impeccable ability to maneuver through and survive so many situations. She’s funny, clever, and thoughtful. Yet you glean much of this indirectly, by seeing what comes of her actions instead of being there with her as she decides what to do.

The whole book is written this way. You learn a lot about the consequences of the characters’ actions, but much less about what compelled them to act in the moment. It’s a bit jarring and sometimes leaves you struggling to turn the pages. At the same time, some of those consequences are so grand that you find yourself wishing you could learn more.

What’s worse is that there are several places where Parker-Chan’s writing is fantastic, especially when writing about love. Parker-Chan can write, but more often than not, it feels like they refuse to.
