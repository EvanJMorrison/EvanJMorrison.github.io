When scientists don't engage with philosophers of science but make philosophical conclusions, they are doomed to repeat their mistakes. If Sagan had only briefly read the logical positivists and some WVO Quine, he would have avoided making many grand-sweeping verificationist claims.

Aside from this philosophical error, Carl Sagan is mostly right; our society engages in many anti-intellectual habits that are difficult to dispel, and this engagement lends itself to unfavorable outcomes. However, I do not think his promise of increased scientific literacy as a cure is as powerful as he believes.

As a scientist myself, I find that scientists within my field (biology) are often just as susceptible to pseudo-scientific beliefs and principles. I think the book is worth the read, but there is better literature on the subject.
